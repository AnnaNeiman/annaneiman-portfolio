The 60-second version

The concept, the screens, and the system that outlasted us.

  1. 1

    The problem

    Most customers never found the streaming apps in their TV plan. We had 2.5 weeks to change that.

  2. 2

    The move

    One card that adapts to each customer's plan, with plain labels like Activate and Upgrade.

  3. 3

    The result

    Upgrades up 37%. Six providers launched with no design rework.

01 · Context

The problem was never the first screen. It was every screen after launch.

Spectrum TV plans included streaming apps like ESPN, Peacock, and Paramount+, and most customers never found them. Competitors had polished marketplaces. We had the partnerships but no single place to show them.

We asked for 10 weeks to build one for a CEO shareholder unveil and got 2.5. The cards still had to put entertainment first, meet accessibility standards at strict character limits, and work on every screen size. With no designer available for each new provider, the system had to scale on its own.

02 · Research

Customers chose by the show and feared a hidden charge.

01

Customers pick by what they want to watch, not which app to pay for. Art leads the card and state language comes second.

02

In an Aug 2025 test with 8 customers, TV customers read “Unlock” as an extra charge. That pushed us toward “Activate.”

03

Accessibility set a 32px minimum logo size, so we chose which provider logos to show.

03 · Key decisions

Every decision names what lost and why.

Decision 1

Chose “Activate” over the exec-favored “Unlock”

Link
✗Rejected
Why it lost

Read as an extra payment.

Customers took “Unlock” as an extra payment, and it was hard to translate into Spanish.

✓Shipped
Why it won

Says exactly what to do.

“Activate” translates cleanly and reads clearly with a screen reader.

The trade-off: we went against what execs wanted, so the case had to hold up. My content-design partner built it with competitive analysis and usability evidence. I built the UI system that carries “Activate” and the full state vocabulary across every card.

↻

Where we changed direction

We spent a day polishing the inherited card, then threw it out.

The old cards crammed dates, upgrade buttons, titles, branding, and promos together. A hierarchy pass didn't fix it, and it still broke on mobile.

So we cut further than felt comfortable: title, date, logo, state. The show art became the hero, and that shipped.

Decision 2

Built one component with 10+ states, not custom cards per provider

Link
✗Rejected
Why it lost

Too much on one card.

Dates, buttons, titles, and promos competed, and it broke on mobile.

✓Shipped
Why it won

One card, 10+ states.

It holds up at every screen size.

One card, five of its 10+ states. The customer's state picks the variant.
Customer state Card says
Included, not yet activated Activate
Activated Available to Watch
Plan tier can improve Upgrade
A la carte option Add
New season available New Season

The trade-off: custom cards launch faster, but each one needs its own contrast and focus fixes, and they never quite match. One shared card took more work up front and almost none per new provider. Contrast and focus were solved once.

Decision 3

Added a gradient overlay and content safe zones to the hero

Link
✗Rejected
Why it lost

Unreadable on busy art.

Text sat directly on rotating images.

✓Shipped
Why it won

Readable on any image.

A gradient keeps text at 4.5:1 contrast on any image.

The result: I designed the overlay and templated it in the CMS, so marketing can swap images without design. My content-design partner set the safe-zone rules: character limits, headline to CTA order, and targeting. The exec got the look, marketing got autonomy, customers got readable text, and accessibility approved it.

04 · Build & validate

A usability test found what the prototype got wrong.

We tested a working prototype with 8 customers in Aug 2025, a week before the launch target. It is a small sample, so we read it as directional. Its main job was to shape the fast-follows that came with launch.

  • Tasks: describe the page, find items, then buy, upgrade, and watch.
  • Finding: 3 of 8 failed the Watch task. “Ready to Watch” implied instant playback but opened an app detail page with the button low on the screen.

Where customers got stuck: the app detail page.

07 · Reflection

I'd bring research in first and launch in phases.

  • Bring research in on day one.
  • Test on mobile earlier.
  • Phase the launch: core swimlanes first, hero carousel and targeting after.
  • Test CTAs by segment, a lesson from my content-design partner.
